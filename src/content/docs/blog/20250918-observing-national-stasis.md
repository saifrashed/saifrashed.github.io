---
title: Observing National Stasis
description: Excessive regulation can lead to bureaucratic paralysis.
---

18 September, 2025

According to Adriaan Mol, founder of Mollie and MessageBird and [worth 3.5 billion dollars](https://www.quotenet.nl/quote-500/a135971/adriaan-mol-bio/), as quoted in the [Parool newspaper](https://www.parool.nl/amsterdam/techondernemer-adriaan-mol-zou-mollie-niet-nog-eens-in-amsterdam-beginnen-de-politiek-behandelt-ons-als-dikke-bankiers~ba461f50/?referrer=https%3A%2F%2Fmtsprout.nl%2F), he would not start his companies in Amsterdam if given another chance. He added that tech companies thrive despite the government, not because of it. In Portugal, the CEO of Cloudflare [stated the following on X](https://x.com/eastdakota/status/1926895319595245750) when asked about his unsatisfaction with Portugal for Cloudflare:

> "Mostly around immigration. But also permitting. General stifling bureaucracy. And a complete lack of a functional airport. Mostly I’m sick of being told things are going to get better if we just invest more only for them to continue to trend worse." — Matthew Prince

A similar sentiment is echoed by ASML, the world’s largest semiconductor producer, which has [threatened to leave the Netherlands](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2024/mar/06/dutch-ministers-trying-to-stop-tech-firm-asml-moving-abroad-over-foreign-labour-fears). This prompted the government to initiate [Plan Beethoven](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/actueel/nieuws/2024/03/28/nederland-investeert-25-miljard-euro-in-sterk-ondernemingsklimaat-voor-microchipsector-brainport-eindhoven), a program aimed at increasing housing and improving the electricity grid to retain the conglomerate, as it provides the Netherlands with a significant position in global politics. The CEO at the time, Peter Wennink, stated that strict immigration policies limit the import of talent from abroad, restricting the company’s growth:

> “We prefer to do that here, but if we cannot get those people here, we will get those people in Eastern Europe, or in Asia, or in the United States.” — Peter Wennink

Additionally, [Reuters reports](https://www.reuters.com/business/asmls-second-quarter-bookings-beat-estimates-2025-07-16/) that ASML cannot guarantee further growth in 2026 due to geopolitical and macroeconomic challenges, such as U.S. tariffs set by President Trump, indicating the risks of overdependence on the U.S. [Restrictions on selling to China](https://www.asml.com/en/news/press-releases/2024/asml-statement-on-updated-us-export-restrictions) also pose challenges for the company, confirming that heavy bureaucracy hampers innovation and growth.

While the importance of rules cannot be understated, excessive regulation can lead to bureaucratic paralysis. A state of stasis in which individuals and organizations cannot grow. A growth mindset requires embracing the risk of failure and a free exchange of ideas, all while maintaining a unifying vision for the future. Until Dutch society becomes courageous in facing the fear of unknown consequences, it will struggle to progress in an increasingly volatile world.
